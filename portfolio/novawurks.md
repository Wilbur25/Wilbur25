# NovaWurks Satellite & Robotic Arm

**Spacecraft hardware from first CAD to orbit: robotic arms, a 750 psi propellant module and reaction wheels for modular "building-block" satellites, assembled by astronauts on the ISS and flown for DARPA.**

| Company | Role | Programs | Years | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NovaWurks · Los Alamitos, CA | Aerospace Engineer II | DARPA Phoenix & SeeMe · NASA SIMPL | 2012–2015 | SolidWorks · 3D printing · CNC |

## The Challenge
NovaWurks pioneered the **HISat (Hyper-Integrated Satlet)**: a roughly 20 × 20 × 10 cm satellite "cell" with its own propulsion, power, communications and attitude control. HISats lock together like building blocks to form larger spacecraft that share power, data and thermal resources. The work supported DARPA's Phoenix and SeeMe programs, which explored in-orbit servicing and rapid-deploy satellites. Every part I designed had to survive launch, work in vacuum through extreme thermal swings, and in the case of the propellant module, hold high pressure safely. Schedules were tight, and I owned each piece of hardware end to end.

## The Design
I did all of the CAD for the mechanical systems I owned:
- **Robotic arms and mechanisms** for harvesting components and aligning optics in orbit
- **A high-pressure propellant module**, built around an aluminum vessel
- **A reaction-wheel system** for pointing and maneuvering
- **Motor and bearing integration** for the actuated assemblies

Choosing space-rated alloys and hard-anodized finishes early reduced manufacturing risk and kept vendors aligned for production.

## Build & Test
I ran the in-house prototyping lab and 3D-printed parts to prove fit and function before committing to metal. Flight parts were machined by outside vendors, and I coordinated the clean-room builds. I drove and tested the motors myself and planned and ran qualification testing, including pressure and environmental tests. I also flew satellite hardware on a zero-gravity research flight.

## The Result
- **The aluminum propellant enclosure was qualified to 750 psi.**
- **SIMPL (NASA / NanoRacks):** the satellite went to the International Space Station in pieces. Astronauts assembled its eight subassemblies (six HISats plus two solar arrays) in microgravity, and it was deployed from the station. It was the first propulsion-capable satellite released from the NanoRacks Kaber deployer.
- **eXCITe (DARPA):** the first DARPA Phoenix spacecraft, built entirely from HISats, launched on a Falcon 9 into sun-synchronous orbit to show that aggregated satlets can survive launch and work together as a spacecraft.
- **I presented Critical Design Reviews to NASA and DARPA** that secured continuation of a **$46M program**.
